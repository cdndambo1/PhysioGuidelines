# PhysioGuidelines – clinical scope and content framework

Updated: 16 November 2026  
Status: Version 1.1 scope map. This file describes planned coverage; it is not a collection of completed clinical practice guidelines.

## Purpose
PhysioGuidelines is a free, international educational reference for physiotherapists and rehabilitation teams. It will provide concise, original summaries that direct readers to the authoritative source guideline, systematic review or professional standard. It is not a substitute for clinical judgement, formal training, local policy or individualised assessment.

## Specialty and condition map
- **Neurology and neurorehabilitation:** stroke; traumatic/acquired brain injury; spinal cord injury; multiple sclerosis; Parkinson’s disease; peripheral neuropathy; intellectual and developmental disabilities; cerebral palsy; spasticity, mobility and participation.
- **Musculoskeletal:** low back and neck pain; radicular pain; osteoarthritis; tendinopathy; shoulder, hip, knee and ankle disorders; persistent pain; exercise and self-management.
- **Orthopaedics:** fractures; joint replacement; ligament/tendon repair; post-operative rehabilitation; mobility restrictions and return to function.
- **Paediatrics:** cerebral palsy; developmental delay; Down syndrome; spina bifida; developmental coordination disorder; torticollis; paediatric respiratory and neuromuscular conditions; transition to adult services.
- **Cardiorespiratory:** COPD; asthma; bronchiectasis; pneumonia; cystic fibrosis; cardiac rehabilitation; heart failure; post-cardiac surgery; airway clearance and exercise.
- **Burns:** acute burn rehabilitation; positioning; contracture prevention; scar/oedema management; splinting principles; mobility and participation.
- **Women’s and pelvic health:** stress, urge and mixed urinary incontinence; pelvic organ prolapse; pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain; antenatal/postnatal rehabilitation; pelvic pain and post-gynaecological surgery.
- **Geriatrics and falls:** falls risk; recurrent falls; frailty; deconditioning; osteoporosis; balance/gait impairment; sarcopenia; post-admission mobility and healthy ageing.
- **Critical care and care transitions:** prehabilitation; peri-operative mobilisation; ICU-acquired weakness; early mobilisation; post-critical illness recovery; discharge planning; early supported discharge and community rehabilitation.
- **Oncology:** cancer-related fatigue; exercise during/after treatment; deconditioning; lymphoedema-related function; bone metastases precautions; post-cancer surgery rehabilitation.
- **Amputee rehabilitation:** pre-operative and pre-prosthetic care; residual-limb management; contracture prevention; prosthetic gait; balance, falls and participation.
- **Vestibular rehabilitation:** benign paroxysmal positional vertigo; unilateral/bilateral vestibular hypofunction; persistent dizziness; balance impairment and vestibular migraine-related rehabilitation considerations.
- **Cross-cutting practice:** outcome measurement; exercise prescription; pain; disability inclusion; communication; supported decision-making; safeguarding; shared decision-making; prevention and health promotion.

## Standard condition-summary template
Every condition entry should contain:
1. Definition, classification and clinical context.
2. Typical presentation and relevant differential considerations.
3. Red flags, contraindications, precautions and referral/escalation pathways.
4. Subjective assessment, including patient goals, function, context and preferences.
5. Objective assessment, including examination and appropriate investigations within scope.
6. Validated outcome measures: purpose, population, administration, scoring/interpretation, measurement properties where available, training/licensing requirements and references.
7. Problem list across impairment, activity and participation.
8. Individualised SMART goals.
9. Evidence-based interventions: indication, rationale, dosage, progression, monitoring, precautions and stop criteria.
10. MDT coordination, education, self-management and caregiver involvement where appropriate.
11. Discharge criteria, follow-up and safety-netting.
12. References, source dates, evidence certainty/recommendation strength as reported by the source, and review metadata.

## Evidence and editorial governance
- Prefer current national/international clinical practice guidelines, systematic reviews and recognised professional standards.
- Link to the original source; write original summaries. Do not reproduce copyrighted or member-only guideline text without permission.
- Preserve the source organisation’s own evidence grading and recommendation wording. Do not invent GRADE ratings or certainty levels.
- Record title, issuing organisation, jurisdiction, publication/update date, date checked, link/DOI, population, recommendation grade (if stated), reviewer and next review date.
- Label content as **planned**, **draft**, **source-checked**, or **clinically reviewed**. Only use “clinically reviewed” after a suitably qualified reviewer has approved the entry and the review is documented.
- Re-check for updates on a defined schedule and when important safety alerts or guideline updates are issued.
- Include an editorial correction route and change log before describing the resource as a governed clinical guideline platform.

## Irish professional and service context
- **Irish Society of Chartered Physiotherapists (ISCP):** the professional body representing Chartered Physiotherapists in Ireland. Its professional resources may include member-only material; link to public resources and obtain permission before using restricted content.
- **CORU:** the statutory regulator for health and social care professionals in Ireland, including physiotherapists. It maintains the register and sets professional standards and requirements.
- **Health Service Executive (HSE):** Ireland’s public health service; its national clinical programmes, models of care and clinical guidelines are relevant to Irish service pathways.
- These organisations have different roles. PhysioGuidelines is independent and must not imply endorsement, approval or ownership by ISCP, CORU or HSE.
- International guidance must be adapted cautiously to local legislation, referral pathways, scope of practice, service availability and population needs.

## Safety and privacy
The app is an educational reference, not a diagnostic or treatment decision system. Do not enter patient-identifiable or confidential information. Apply consent, safeguarding, professional scope, local escalation procedures and individual clinical judgement.
